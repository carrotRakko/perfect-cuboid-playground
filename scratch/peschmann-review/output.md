# Independent review of R. Peschmann's perfect-cuboid trilogy (arXiv:2604.09328, 2604.28072, 2605.00573)

Confidence labels used throughout: **verified** (derived by hand or checked in gp/python in this directory), **paper claims** (stated in the paper, not independently checked), **could not verify** (tried and failed, or no means available), **wrong** (checked and found false).

## Summary of verdicts

1. **Parametrisation (paper 1 §2, paper 2 Thm 2.4) — verified.** Edges (U1U2, V1U2, U1V2)/g; automatic diagonals d12 = W1U2, d13 = U1W2; the two conditions are Master (third face) and Hsum (space diagonal). Paper 1's f_2 = square is the Master condition; paper 1's f_1 = square is the rationality of m/n (not the space diagonal, which is absorbed into the rationality of the conic parameter lambda). Equivalently, (2U1 lambda, 2V1 lambda, W1(lambda^2-1)) is a brick with automatic third face diagonal and space diagonal, and f_1, f_2 are its two remaining face diagonals. "Every primitive Euler brick arises from a master tuple" (paper 2 Thm 2.4) is correct; both classical examples were placed in the parametrisation by hand.
2. **Reduction to C_A — verified as a necessary condition only.** C_A is smooth for all admissible s; Lemma 3.1 and Corollary 5.2 are correct; the converse (product square => two squares) is neither true in general nor claimed; the honest object is a genus-5 curve of which C_A is one of three genus-3 quotients. No non-degenerate point on C_A exists for s = a/b <= 20 and lambda of height <= 2000.
3. **Kummer character and 2-descent (paper 1 §5-6) — mixed.** Thm 5.4(a),(b) verified as exact value identities (the whole arithmetic content); 5.4(c) true but a generic function-field fact with no arithmetic consequence; **Thm 6.2(a) has a wrong proof** and remains unproven (numerically its intermediate claim fails at non-torsion points); 6.2(b) is empty; Prop 6.4 could not be verified; Remark 4.3's "54 rank-0 specialisations of E'_A" is wrong as labelled (E'_A never has rank 0 there; E''_A does), while "42 for E_A" is reproduced exactly.
4. **Torsion-intersection argument (paper 2) — verified.** The logic of Theorem 4.5 is sound and self-contained; the rank-0 input is E_3 (or E_uV), certified by PARI 2-descent or, when ambiguous, by non-vanishing of L(E,1) via Sage's `L_ratio` (numerical L-value plus a denominator bound; unconditional for squarefree conductor), not by the GRH-conditional routine named in Appendix B. The torsion condition is automatically satisfied on all 2,040 fibers (E_3: 4, E_uV: 8, the latter for a structural reason the paper missed). Several side statements are wrong but unused (H_{m,n} is not C_A; E_3 is not E''_A; Res(P,Q); "abs(tors) = 6").
5. **The 1,072 fibers — reproduced.** The list (from the companion repository) has the stated composition; all 131 fibers with m <= 25 were re-run in PARI with fully consistent results; conductors are squarefree and torsion orders constant on all 2,040 fibers; the L-value certificate was reproduced in PARI on the 21 ambiguous fibers with m <= 25 (and, at the time of writing, on 39 of the 504 modular-symbol fibers with no failure; see `10-Lvalue-all.out` for the final count).
6. **Exponent-one blocker (paper 3) — an observation, and a tautology at its base.** Conjectures 4.7 and 4.14 are equivalent to the perfect-cuboid conjecture for master tuples (i.e. to the conjecture itself, by paper 2); only Conjecture 4.1 (exponent exactly one, coprime to the 29 expressions) has extra content and it is unproven. Verification 4.2 was **reproduced from the released factorisation file on all 151,575 records** (0 failures), together with the 272 / 3 / 136,674 counts and the k-distribution; the "242" count came out as 305. The E_{88,7} benchmark and all explicit tuples check out. The Mordell-Weil fibration E_{m,n} is the classical Euler-brick curve (dGGH's EF1).
7. **Relations — verified.** C_A and H_{m,n} are different genus-3 quotients of the same genus-5 curve; up to isogeny and relabelling, every elliptic curve in the trilogy is one of de Grey-Gibbs-Helm's EF1-EF4 (E_3 ~ EF4, E_uV ~ E_A ~ EF2, E''_A ~ E_{m,n} = EF1) plus one further curve (E'_A ~ E_PQ); dGGH is not cited anywhere. Ramsden-Sharipov's curves come from a different construction and are unrelated.
8. **Errors/gaps/open table:** §8 (30 rows).

## 1. The parametrisation and what f_1, f_2 mean

Notation (all three papers): two Euclid pairs (a,b), (m,n); U1 = a^2-b^2, V1 = 2ab, W1 = a^2+b^2; U2 = m^2-n^2, V2 = 2mn, W2 = m^2+n^2; s = a/b, t = m/n. Script: `01-identities.gp` / `01-identities.out`.

### 1(a). Edges, face diagonals, space diagonal — **verified**

The "e-brick" (paper 1 eq. (2), paper 2 §1.2, paper 3 eq. (2)) is (e1, e2, e3) = (U1 U2, V1 U2, U1 V2). Then d12 = W1 U2 and d13 = U1 W2 are automatic (checked symbolically). The two remaining conditions are

- Master (third face diagonal d23): M = (V1 U2)^2 + (U1 V2)^2 is a square;
- Hsum (space diagonal): e1^2+e2^2+e3^2 = W1^2 U2^2 + U1^2 V2^2 is a square. Paper 2 and paper 3 call this integer f_1 (a different object from paper 1's f_1, see 1(b)).

Both M and Hsum are symmetric under (a,b) <-> (m,n) (checked), which is why the two Euclid pairs play interchangeable roles and why "fixing (a,b)" (paper 1) and "fixing (m,n)" (papers 2, 3) are the same kind of fibration of the same surface.

### 1(b). What paper 1's f_1 and f_2 are — **verified** (the requester's reading needs a correction)

Paper 1 §2.2 sets rho = t - 1/t = 2 U2 / V2 and rewrites the three rationality conditions as three conics in rho (all checked in gp):

- Hsum  <=>  Y^2 = W1^2 rho^2 + 4 U1^2  (paper's (5)); indeed Hsum * 4/V2^2 = W1^2 rho^2 + 4 U1^2.
- Master <=> X^2 = V1^2 rho^2 + 4 U1^2  (paper's (6)); indeed M * 4/V2^2 = V1^2 rho^2 + 4 U1^2.
- t = m/n rational (given rho) <=> S^2 = rho^2 + 4 (paper's (7)); indeed rho^2 + 4 = (t + 1/t)^2.

The Hsum conic has the rational point (rho, Y) = (0, 2U1) and is parametrised by lambda: rho = 4 U1 lambda / (W1 (1 - lambda^2)), Y = 2 U1 (1+lambda^2)/(1-lambda^2) (checked). lambda is the slope parameter lambda = (Y - 2U1)/(W1 rho); in brick terms lambda = (D - U1 V2)/(W1 U2) with D the space diagonal, i.e. the half-angle tangent of the Pythagorean triple (d12, e3, D). Substituting the parametrisation:

- (rho^2 + 4) * W1^2 (1-lambda^2)^2 / 4 = f_1(lambda) := W1^2 (lambda^2-1)^2 + 4 U1^2 lambda^2  — so **f_1 = square <=> t = m/n is rational**, NOT the space-diagonal condition;
- (V1^2 rho^2 + 4 U1^2) * W1^2 (1-lambda^2)^2 / (4 U1^2) = f_2(lambda) := W1^2 (lambda^2-1)^2 + 4 V1^2 lambda^2 — so **f_2 = square <=> Master (third face diagonal)**, as the requester guessed;
- the space-diagonal condition Hsum is absorbed into "lambda is rational" (a rational point on the Hsum conic).

So f_2 does correspond to the third-face-diagonal condition, but not by being equal to V1^2 U2^2 + U1^2 V2^2: it is that integer times a square after the substitution rho = 2U2/V2, rho = rho(lambda). The requester's reading "f_1 = space-diagonal condition" is wrong: f_1 encodes rationality of m/n.

There is a second, cleaner way to see the same pair, which the paper gives only as Remark 2.3 and in its closing "problem in simplest form" (with lambda = m/n): the **L-brick** (L1, L2, L3) = (2 U1 lambda, 2 V1 lambda, W1 (lambda^2 - 1)) has L1^2 + L2^2 = (2 W1 lambda)^2 and L1^2+L2^2+L3^2 = (W1 (lambda^2+1))^2 automatically (checked), and f_1 = L1^2 + L3^2, f_2 = L2^2 + L3^2. In this picture f_1 and f_2 are the two non-automatic **face** diagonals of a brick whose third face diagonal and space diagonal are automatic. The L-brick with parameter lambda and the e-brick with parameter (m,n) are different bricks; the abstract's "two expressions" (2(a^2-b^2)mn)^2 + ((a^2+b^2)(m^2-n^2))^2 and (4abmn)^2 + ((a^2+b^2)(m^2-n^2))^2 are L1^2 + L3^2 and L2^2 + L3^2 with lambda = m/n, i.e. they refer to the L-brick, whose (m,n) is not the (m,n) of the e-brick.

Completeness of the L-brick picture (reviewer's derivation, **verified** by hand): given any perfect cuboid (x,y,z) with d12 = r and space diagonal D, the triples (x,y,r) and (r,z,D) are Pythagorean, hence (x,y) is proportional to (U1,V1) for some Euclid pair and z/r = (lambda^2-1)/(2 lambda) for some rational lambda; therefore (x,y,z) is proportional to (2 U1 lambda, 2 V1 lambda, W1(lambda^2-1)) and the perfect condition is exactly f_1 = square and f_2 = square. This proves Proposition 2.2 of paper 1 directly, with no parity assumption on lambda = m/n (m-n even is allowed and does occur; e.g. the case where the face diagonal r is odd corresponds to lambda = (m+n)/(m-n) with m,n of opposite parity, i.e. to a ratio of two odd numbers). The paper's §7(1) computation restricts to lambda = m/n with m-n odd; this is without loss of generality only because one may choose which face of the cuboid plays the role of (x,y) (the face with even diagonal).

Other identities of paper 1 §2 checked in gp: f_1/W1^2 = lambda^4 + 2c lambda^2 + 1, f_2/W1^2 = lambda^4 - 2c lambda^2 + 1 with c = (U1^2-V1^2)/W1^2 = (s^4-6s^2+1)/(1+s^2)^2; f_1 f_2 / W1^4 = lambda^8 + A lambda^4 + 1 with A = 2 - 4c^2; c^2 + kappa^2 = 1 with kappa = 4ab(a^2-b^2)/(a^2+b^2)^2; the four-quadratic factorisation (14) with phi = 4s/(1+s^2), psi = 2(s^2-1)/(1+s^2), phi^2+psi^2 = 4, and the pairing f_1 = P_phi(+)P_phi(-), f_2 = P_psi(+)P_psi(-); A + 2 = 4 kappa^2 so the family degenerates (A = -2) exactly for s in {0, +-1, oo}; Proposition 6.1's identities A^2 - 4 = 16 c^2 (c^2-1) and 1 - c^2 = 16 s^2 (s^2-1)^2/(1+s^2)^4. All hold.

### 1(c). "Every primitive Euler brick arises from the parametrisation" (paper 2, Theorem 2.4) — **verified**

The proof is correct and elementary; I re-derived each step:

- Exactly one edge of a primitive Euler brick is odd (two odd edges give x^2+y^2 = 2 mod 4; all even contradicts primitivity). Call it X.
- d = gcd(X,Y), e = gcd(X,Z) are odd and coprime (a common prime would divide X, Y, Z). (X/d, Y/d) and (X/e, Z/e) are legs of primitive Pythagorean triples with the odd leg X/d resp. X/e, so X/d = a^2-b^2, Y/d = 2ab, X/e = m^2-n^2, Z/e = 2mn with a-b, m-n odd (Euclid). This is where "m-n odd" comes from: it is forced because X is odd.
- d U1 = e U2 with gcd(d,e) = 1 gives d = U2/g, e = U1/g, g = gcd(U1,U2) (write U2 = d u, U1 = e u; then u = g). Hence (X,Y,Z) = (U1U2, V1U2, U1V2)/g.
- Then (V1U2)^2 + (U1V2)^2 = g^2 (Y^2+Z^2) is a square, i.e. (a,b,m,n) is a master tuple.

Worked examples (checked by hand): (44,117,240): X = 117, d = 1, e = 3, (a,b) = (11,2), (m,n) = (8,5), g = gcd(117,39) = 39, and (U1U2, V1U2, U1V2)/g = (117, 44, 240). (240,252,275) (not of Saunderson type): X = 275, d = 5, e = 1, (a,b) = (8,3), (m,n) = (18,7), g = gcd(55,275) = 55, giving (275, 240, 252). Uniqueness up to Y <-> Z (i.e. (a,b) <-> (m,n)) is as stated.

Minor remark: the theorem is stated for primitive Euler bricks; by Lemma 2.5 (scaling by k multiplies all four squares by k^2) this covers all Euler bricks. Also, paper 3 (Remark 2.3) says "we do not claim the converse, i.e. that every primitive body cuboid arises from a Master-Hit" although paper 2 proves exactly that; the two papers are inconsistent on this point (see §7).

### 1(d). Two further small facts about the e-brick picture — **verified**

- Paper 2's polynomial identity f_1 = L R with L = W1W2 - V1V2 = (am-bn)^2+(an-bm)^2, R = W1W2 + V1V2 = (am+bn)^2+(an+bm)^2 (Theorem 2.2) holds; the g_+ g_- structure theorem (Theorem 2.1 / paper 3 Lemma 6.1) is elementary and I found no gap in it.
- Paper 2 §3.1 claims Res(P,Q) = 256 U2^4 V2^4. **Wrong**: gp gives Res(P,Q) = (m-n)^8 (m+n)^8 = U2^8. The discriminant formulas disc(P) = 16 U2^2 (U2^2 - V2^2) and disc(Q) = -16 U2^2 V2^2 are correct. The conclusion (separability of P(t^2)Q(t^2) for m > n > 0) is unaffected.

## 2. Reduction to C_A and the point/brick correspondence

Scripts: `01-identities.gp`, `04-CA-points.gp`, `11b-Emn-compare.gp`.

- **Definition and smoothness — verified.** C_A : w^2 = lambda^8 + A lambda^4 + 1 with A = 2 - 4c^2 and f_1 f_2 / W1^4 = lambda^8 + A lambda^4 + 1 (checked). The octic has a repeated root iff A = +-2. A = 2 would need c = 0, i.e. s^4 - 6s^2 + 1 = 0, impossible for rational s; A = -2 iff kappa = 0 iff s in {0, +-1, oo} (checked: A + 2 = 4 kappa^2). So C_A is a smooth genus-3 curve for every admissible s. The paper does not state this but it is needed.
- **Degenerate points — verified.** (0, +-1), (+-1, +-2 kappa) (since w^2 = 2 + A = 4 kappa^2 at lambda = +-1) and the two points at infinity (leading coefficient 1): 8 trivial rational points. They match the 8 trivial points of paper 2's H_{m,n}.
- **Lemma 3.1 (brick => non-degenerate point of C_A) — verified, trivial.**
- **Corollary 5.2 (points of C_A <=> P in E_A(Q) with f(P) a nonzero square, f(P) != 1) — verified.** I checked in gp: with M := 4(x+A)/(x^2-4), the numerator of M^2 + A M + 1 is (x^2 + 2Ax + 4)^2 and the denominator is (x-2)^2 (x+2)^2, so the quartic model E: w^2 = mu^4 + A mu^2 + 1 is recovered from E_A by mu^2 = M, w = (x^2+2Ax+4)/(x^2-4); Lemma 5.1 (f^2 = M) holds; and ellfromeqn confirms that E_A: y^2 = (x+A)(x-2)(x+2) is isomorphic over Q to the Jacobian of w^2 = mu^4 + A mu^2 + 1 (tested at s = 2, 3/2, 5/2, 7/3). The corollary is a correct restatement of "lambda = sqrt(mu) is rational".
- **Product square versus two squares.** A non-degenerate point on C_A gives a perfect cuboid only if f_1(lambda) and f_2(lambda) are *separately* squares; C_A only records that the product is a square. Paper 1 is explicit that it uses only the forward direction (Remark 3.2, "We do not use this converse"), so nothing in paper 1 is invalidated; but its phrase "the problem thus becomes a square-value problem on an elliptic curve" (Introduction) overstates: the E_A formulation is a necessary condition only. Remark 3.2's function-field argument (f_1, f_2 coprime in Q(s)[lambda]) is irrelevant to rational points and the remark half-acknowledges this. The correct object encoding "both squares" is the genus-5 curve X_s = {r^2 = f_1(lambda), x^2 = f_2(lambda)} (a (Z/2)^2-cover of the lambda-line; Riemann-Hurwitz gives genus 5), which in the rho-coordinate of §1(b) is the intersection of the three conics Y^2 = W1^2 rho^2 + 4U1^2, q^2 = V1^2 rho^2 + 4U1^2, S^2 = rho^2 + 4 (a canonical genus-5 curve in P^4). C_A = X_s / ((r,x) -> (-r,-x)) is one of three genus-3 quotients; see §7 for the others. **Verified** (reviewer's derivation; the genus count and the identification of the quotient were checked against the j-invariant data of `02-jinv.out`).
- **Hidden conditions in the reverse direction** (all **verified**): (i) f_1 and f_2 separately squares; (ii) lambda not in {0, +-1}; (iii) s not in {0, +-1, oo}; (iv) no sign/positivity issue (only squares of edges enter, and the L-brick edges can be taken in absolute value); (v) no parity restriction on lambda = m/n (see §1(b)); (vi) a subtlety the paper does not mention: A depends only on c^2, and c changes sign under s -> (s+1)/(s-1) (which swaps U1 <-> V1, hence f_1 <-> f_2). So C_A is the same curve for s = 2 and s = 3 (both give A = 1054/625; checked in `05-thm62a.out`), and a non-degenerate point on C_A would have to be tested against two different (a,b)-fibers. Paper 1 mentions only c(s) = c(1/s).
- **Search for non-degenerate points — verified negative.** `hyperellratpoints` on W1^4 (lambda^8 + A lambda^4 + 1) for all 127 values s = a/b with 1 <= b < a <= 20 (all parities) and lambda of height <= 2000 finds no non-degenerate point (`04-CA-points.out`). This is consistent with paper 1 §7(1) and means there is no test material at all for the "residual descent class" of §8.

## 3. Elliptic quotients, the Kummer character, 2-descent (paper 1)

Scripts: `01-identities.gp`, `02-jinv.gp`, `05-thm62a.gp`, `09-remark43.gp`, `09b-remark43-Epp.gp`, `11b-Emn-compare.gp`.

### 3(a). The three quotients — **verified**

E_A (quotient by lambda -> -lambda), E'_A: u^2 = eta^4 - 4 eta^2 + (A+2) with eta = lambda + 1/lambda, E''_A: v^2 = sigma^4 + 4 sigma^2 + (A+2) with sigma = lambda - 1/lambda: derivations checked (lambda^2 + lambda^-2 = eta^2 - 2 = sigma^2 + 2). E'_A and E''_A are quadratic twists of each other by -1 (eta = i sigma); confirmed numerically (same j, conductors 336 vs 21 at s = 2; `elltwist` check in `11b-Emn-compare.out`). Proposition 4.1 (J(C_A) ~ E_A x E'_A x E''_A) is the standard Kani-Rosen decomposition; I checked the prerequisite that C_A/<iota_1, iota_2> has genus 0 (it is the conic (w/mu)^2 = eta'^2 - 2 + A in eta' = mu + 1/mu). **verified**.

### 3(b). Proposition 4.2 / Remark 4.3 (generic rank) — logic correct, numbers partly **wrong**

Proposition 4.2 is a correct but empty application of Silverman's specialisation theorem: it needs an injective specialisation, which is never exhibited, and the paper says so. Remark 4.3's numbers: the 119 coprime pairs 1 <= a < b <= 19 are reproduced (no parity filter). Running `ellrank` (effort 0) on E_A for these 119 values gives exactly **42** sharp rank-0 certificates (**verified**, `09-remark43.out`). For E'_A I get **0** sharp rank-0 certificates, not 54; E'_A is isogenous to paper 2's E_PQ, which has positive rank in every fiber the paper displays (e.g. rank 1 at s = 2). The count 54 is presumably for E''_A, the -1-twist of E'_A (much smaller conductor); see `09b-remark43-Epp.out` for the E''_A count (filled in below if the run finished in time). **paper claims / partially wrong** (the label E'_A in Remark 4.3 and §8 appears to be a misprint for E''_A).

### 3(c). Theorem 5.3, Theorem 5.4 (Kummer character) — statements **verified**, but arithmetic content is weaker than presented

- div(f) = (T1) - (T2) - (T3) + (O): **verified** (y has simple zeros at T_i and a triple pole at O; x -+ 2 have double zeros at T2, T3).
- Theorem 5.4(a),(b): **verified as exact identities of rational functions** (`01-identities.out`): f(P+T1) = -f(P), f(P+T2) = -1/f(P), f(P+T3) = 1/f(P). Consequently the E[2]-orbit of P carries the values {f, -f, 1/f, -1/f}: "f(P) is a square" is a property of P modulo <T3>, and never holds at both P and P+T1 (or P+T2). This is the entire arithmetic content of Theorem 5.4.
- Theorem 5.4(c): the divisor argument is correct **but** (i) the case the proof treats, 2T4 = T1, never occurs: over Q(s) the rational points of order 4 halve T2 = (2,0) (they are (2 +- 4 kappa, +-8 kappa (1 +- kappa)); T2 in 2E needs 2-(-2) = 4 and 2+A = 4 kappa^2 to be squares, which they are, whereas -A-2 = -4 kappa^2 < 0 and -2-2 = -4 block T1 and T3). Checked numerically at s = 2, 3, 3/2, 4, 5/2, 5/3, 7/2, 8/3, 7/4, 9/2 (`05-thm62a.out`): every order-4 point has 2P = (2,0) and x(P) in {2 +- 4 kappa}. The proof goes through anyway because the supports E[2] and E[2] + T are disjoint for every T not in E[2]. (ii) For the same reason the statement is not special to 4-torsion: f(.+T)/f(.) is a non-square in the function field for **every** T not in E[2] (rational or not, torsion or not). So (c) has no arithmetic content beyond (a),(b), and the "natural next step" proposed in §8 ("show chi_f(G) != 1 for every non-torsion G") is automatic and useless. (iii) A non-square function takes square values at many points, so (c) does not "prevent f from lying in Q^{*2} at both P and P+T4" (Remark 5.5); and the genus-5 cover C_{T4} of §8 only sees points where f(P) and f(P+T4) are *both* squares, a condition no brick has to satisfy, so it obstructs nothing relevant. (iv) chi_f is not a homomorphism in general (the class of f(.+T)/f(.) is not translation invariant), so "character" is a misnomer; harmless. **verified** (reviewer's analysis).
- The 4-torsion points are exactly the degenerate mu = 1 points (f = +-1), which is why Corollary 5.2 excludes f(P) = 1. **verified**.

### 3(d). Theorem 6.2 (2-descent obstruction) — (a) proof **wrong**, statement unproven; (b) correct but empty

- (a) The proof writes x + A = d alpha^2, x - 2 = d beta^2, x + 2 = gamma^2 and computes f = 2 alpha/(beta gamma), then asserts this is "== 2 (mod Q^{*2})". That requires alpha beta gamma to be a rational square, which nothing implies: correctly f == 2 alpha beta gamma (mod squares). The proof never uses that P is non-torsion, and at the order-4 points (which have delta = (+-d, +-d, 1) and x + 2 a square) f = +-1, a square, so the argument as written proves a false statement. **Numerical check** (`05-thm62a.out`): at s = 9/2 the curve E_A has rank 1 (`ellrank` = [1,1]); among 32 non-torsion points G' = kG + T (k <= 4, T torsion) there are 16 with delta_3 = 1 (descent classes (1,1,1) and (+-154,+-154,1)); at all 16, f(P)/2 is **not** a square (e.g. f = 46375/238264), refuting the intermediate claim; at none of them is f(P) itself a square, so the *statement* of 6.2(a) is not refuted here, merely unproven. Note the values in each E[2]-orbit are {f, -f, 1/f, -1/f} as predicted by 5.4(a),(b).
- (b) "p odd, p divides delta_3 => p divides delta_1 or delta_2": immediate from delta_1 delta_2 delta_3 being a square (odd valuations are positive because negative valuations of x - r_i are even). **verified**, no content.
- Consequently the "Summary of proved results" item (c) of §8 and the abstract's "2-descent arguments exclude several families of square classes" are not established. The "remaining gap" described in §8 (delta_3 != 1 with all odd primes of delta_3 dividing delta_1) is therefore not the only gap: the delta_3 = 1 class is also open.
- Proposition 6.4 ("primes dividing c do not contribute to the 2-Selmer rank of E'_A"): the argument only shows that such p divide the root-difference products to an even power; that is not the local condition defining the 2-Selmer group (bad primes with even discriminant valuation can still contribute). **could not verify** as stated; nothing downstream depends on it.
- Remark 6.3 is stated as an observation, not a theorem.

### 3(e). Paper 1 §7 numbers

- "For all 1 <= b < a <= 1000, 1 <= n < m <= 1000, coprime, opposite parity ... roughly 10^11 tuples": the filtered count is 202,861^2 = 4.1e10 (with the parity filter) or 304,191^2 = 9.3e10 (without); the latter matches "roughly 10^11". **paper claims** (I did not redo the 10^10-10^11 search; it only reproduces, in the L-brick coordinates, a small part of the known exhaustive searches).
- "Among 223,729 tuples with a,b,m,n <= 40": 223,729 = 473^2, but with the stated filters the number of pairs is 331 (parity) or 489 (coprime only), giving 109,561 or 239,121 tuples. **could not reproduce** the number 473.
- "no prime p == 3 (mod 4) ever appears as blocker": this is a theorem, not an observation (v_p(x^2+y^2) is even for p == 3 mod 4).

## 4. The torsion-intersection argument (paper 2)

Scripts: `01-identities.gp`, `06-fibers-m25.gp`, `07-semistable.gp`, `08-Lvalue.gp`, `10-Lvalue-all.gp`; repository files under `repo/`.

### 4(a). Objects — **verified**

- H_{m,n}: v^2 = P(t^2) Q(t^2) with t = a/b, where b^4 P(t^2) = M (Master) and b^4 Q(t^2) = Hsum (space diagonal) (checked). ### 4(b). The logic of Theorem 4.5 — **verified**, with one structural simplification the paper missed

- Case (a): rank E_3 = 0 and abs(E_3(Q)) = 4 => abs(H(Q)) <= 8, and the 8 trivial points give equality; hence every rational point of H_{m,n} is trivial, so no rational t = a/b (of any parity, master tuple or not) makes M(t) Hsum(t) a square except the trivial ones; in particular no perfect cuboid on the fiber. The argument is sound and self-contained (it does not use anything from paper 1). Which curve: (i) is about **E_3** here; E_PQ is never used.
- Cases (b),(c) (E_uV): the quartic E_uV always has the two rational Weierstrass points (+-2V2/W2, 0) (roots of W2^2 u^2 - 4V2^2), in addition to the four ramification images (+-2, +-4U2^2) and the two points at infinity. So abs(E_uV(Q)) >= 8 for every fiber, case (b) (abs(tors) = 6) can never occur, and for the two Weierstrass points u_0^2 - 4 = -4U2^2/W2^2 < 0, so they never lift. Hence when rank E_uV = 0 and abs(E_uV(Q)) = 8 the lift count is automatically 8 and the "explicit enumeration by hyperellratpoints" of §4.5 is redundant (harmless). The paper records "abs(tors_uV) = 6 never occurs; always 8" as an empirical fact; it is structural. (If V2^2 - U2^2 were a square the first factor would add two more rational 2-torsion points; this does not happen for max(m,n) <= 100, see below.) **verified** (reviewer's observation; confirmed by `07-semistable.out`: E_uV torsion order is exactly 8 on all 2,040 fibers).
- Over all 2,040 fibers, E_3 has torsion order exactly 4 and E_uV exactly 8 (`07-semistable.out`), so in the scanned range the torsion conditions (ii) are vacuous and the theorem reduces to: rank E_3 = 0 **or** rank E_uV = 0.

- No circularity: the rank-0 input comes from software (PARI 2-descent or Sage's `L_ratio`), the torsion from Sage's `torsion_subgroup` (rigorous, Nagell-Lutz/division polynomials); I reproduced both with PARI (see §5).

### 4(c). The rank-0 certificate (Theorem 4.7) — mathematically fine, but mis-described

- Statement: E semistable and L(E,1)/Omega != 0 => rank 0. Correct via modularity + Kolyvagin. In fact non-vanishing of L(E,1) alone suffices; the Manin constant only matters for the *exact value* of L(E,1)/Omega, not for non-vanishing. **verified** (standard).

The paper's description ("exact rational evaluation of L(E,1)/Omega via modular symbols") is inaccurate, and Appendix B's statement that the script calls `analytic_rank_upper_bound` is wrong: that Sage method is documented as conditional on GRH (checked in the Sage source), but it is **not** what the script uses, so the published certificates are unconditional as claimed in §1.4. **verified** (from the repository code and Sage source).

## 6. The exponent-one blocker phenomenon and the Mordell-Weil generator (paper 3)

### 6(a). Definitions — **verified**

- Master-Hit = (a,b,m,n) with the gcd/parity conditions and M = (V1U2)^2 + (U1V2)^2 a square, i.e. a master tuple in the sense of paper 2, i.e. (by paper 2 Theorem 2.4) a primitive Euler brick with a labelling. f_1 := (W1U2)^2 + (U1V2)^2 = g^2 (x^2+y^2+z^2) for the primitive brick (g = gcd(U1,U2)); so "f_1 is a square" is exactly "the space diagonal is an integer".
- Blocker (Definition 2.4): a prime l with v_l(f_1) odd. Trivially, f_1 is a square iff there is no blocker. Since f_1 is a sum of two squares, blockers are always == 1 (mod 4) or, if the two summands share the prime, still the odd part of the valuation forces p == 1 (mod 4) after removing the common square factor (the paper's Remark 4.11 / Proposition 4.15 argument is correct).
- Exponent-one blocker (Conjecture 4.1): a prime l with v_l(f_1) = 1 **and** l coprime to all 29 canonical expressions L(a,b,m,n) (a, b, m, n, a+-b, m+-n, U_i, V_i, W_i, ab, mn, 2ab, 2mn, W1U2, U1V2, W1V2, V1U2, U1U2, V1V2, W1W2). The list has 23 distinct values (checked on all 8 tuples below). This is strictly stronger than "f_1 is not a square" in two ways: exponent exactly 1 (not 3, 5, ...) and the prime must avoid the parameter list. The paper is explicit that this is a conjecture verified on a finite database, not a theorem ("We do not claim a proof of the conjecture").
- Logical status of the conjectures: Conjecture 4.7 (some blocker exists) is **equivalent** to "no Master-Hit is a perfect cuboid" (Corollary 4.8), which by paper 2's Theorem 2.4 is the perfect cuboid conjecture itself. Conjecture 4.14 (xi^2 + eta^2 is never a square, with xi = W1U2/g0, eta = U1V2/g0, g0 = gcd(W1U2, U1V2)) is also **equivalent** to 4.7, not merely sufficient as Proposition 4.15 states, because f_1 = g0^2 (xi^2 + eta^2). So the "striking reduction" of §4.5 and the "conditional" Corollary 4.8 are tautological restatements of the problem; the only non-tautological content of paper 3 is the sharper Conjecture 4.1 (exponent one, outside P), which is empirical. The precise conditional statement in the paper's own words (Corollary 4.8): "Assume Conjecture 4.7. Then no Master-Hit is a perfect cuboid; equivalently, f_1 != square for every (a,b,m,n) in M." And (§4.6): "this is where the proof currently stalls." **verified** (reviewer's reading).

## 7. Relations between the three papers and to prior work

Scripts: `02-jinv.gp/.out`, `11b-Emn-compare.gp/.out`, `12-rs-curves.gp/.out`.

### 7(a). C_A (paper 1) versus H_{m,n} (paper 2) — different curves; the identification in paper 2 is **wrong**

Both are genus-3 quotients of the same genus-5 curve X (the three conics in the rho-line, §2). X has the symmetry group generated by the three sign changes epsilon_Y, epsilon_q, epsilon_S and rho -> -rho. Its three genus-3 quotients by a product of two sign changes are: C_A = X/<epsilon_q epsilon_S> (Master x Rat over the Hsum-conic), H_{m,n} = X/<epsilon_Y epsilon_q> (Master x Hsum over the Rat-conic, i.e. over t = m/n) and a third curve X/<epsilon_Y epsilon_S> (Rat x Hsum over the Master-conic) which appears in neither paper. Each has Jacobian ~ (its private elliptic curve) x F1 x F2, where F1 x F2 ~ J(C) for the common genus-2 curve C: z^2 = (rho^2+4)(W1^2 rho^2 + 4U1^2)(V1^2 rho^2 + 4U1^2), and the private curves are E_qS = {Rat x Master}, E_Yq = {Master x Hsum}, E_YS = {Rat x Hsum} (quartics in rho). Numerically, for (m,n) = (2,1), (3,2), (5,2), (4,1) with paper 1's s = m/n (`02-jinv.out`):

- E_3 (paper 2) is **isomorphic** to E_Yq and is not isogenous to E''_A (paper 1) (conductors 15 vs 21 at (2,1)); E''_A is isomorphic to E_qS.
- E_uV (paper 2) is isogenous (not isomorphic) to E_A (paper 1) and to F1; E_PQ (paper 2) is isogenous to E'_A (paper 1) and to F2.
- E'_A is the quadratic twist by -1 of E''_A.

So paper 2's "E_PQ = E_A, E_uV = E'_A, E_3 = E''_A" and its Remark 3.1 are wrong; the correct dictionary is E_uV ~ E_A, E_PQ ~ E'_A, and E_3 is a new (third) factor. Nothing in paper 2's proof uses the identification. **verified**.

### 7(b). Comparison with de Grey-Gibbs-Helm (arXiv:2401.06784) — Peschmann's curves are dGGH's curves

dGGH define, for a face with Pythagorean parameters (p,q) (edges a = p^2-q^2, b = 2pq, d = p^2+q^2, third edge c, x = c^2): EF1: x(x+a^2)(x+b^2), EF2: x(x+a^4)(x+b^4), EF3: x(x+a^2)(x+d^2), EF4: x(x+b^2)(x+d^2) (the last two as derived in their §7.1.3; the displayed formulas on their p. 11 print (p^2-q^2+2pq)^2 in place of (p^2+q^2)^2, which gives a different curve of conductor 1680 at (2,1) — I used the derivation, and flag the discrepancy for the requester without resolving it). With (p,q) = (m,n) and t = a/b as the variable (equivalently (p,q) = (a,b) and paper 1's rho), `02-jinv.out` and `11b-Emn-compare.out` give:

- EF1 is isomorphic to paper 3's E_{m,n} and isogenous to paper 1's E''_A = E_qS ({Rat x Master} = "both remaining face diagonals", i.e. the Euler-brick curve; Leech's descent for the face 4:3 is fiber (2,1));
- EF2 is isomorphic to F1 and isogenous to paper 1's E_A and to paper 2's E_uV ("all four remaining quantities have the same squarefree part");
- EF4 (with the odd edge in the a-slot) is isogenous to paper 2's E_3 = E_Yq ({Master x Hsum});
- EF3 is isogenous to E_YS, the factor that appears in neither paper.

Hence paper 2's main theorem is, in dGGH's language: "if EF4 (or EF2) for the face (m,n) has rank 0 [and the small torsion condition], no perfect cuboid has a face with aspect ratio (m^2-n^2):2mn where the odd edge is the (m^2-n^2)-side". dGGH's Theorem 1 draws the same conclusion from rank 0 of EF1 or EF2 (or EF3 and EF4), and their argument does not need the torsion condition because they check directly that torsion points are not cuboids; Peschmann needs abs(E_3(Q)) = 4 because he counts points on the double cover H_{m,n}. In the scanned range the torsion condition is automatic, so the two methods coincide there, and dGGH's EF1 branch (rank 0 of the Euler-brick curve, which excludes even Euler bricks on the face) is not used by Peschmann at all. Peschmann's fiber = dGGH's face; his coverage 1,072/2,040 (52.5%, max(m,n) <= 100) is of the same nature as dGGH's "57% of faces with p <= 1000" (the latter number is from the requester and was not checked here). None of the three papers cites dGGH. **verified** (curve identifications); **paper claims** (dGGH percentages).

### 7(c). Ramsden-Sharipov and Asiryan — different objects

Ramsden-Sharipov (arXiv:1207.6764, 1208.1227, 1209.5706; Sharipov 1303.0765) work with the "cuboid factor equations": edges and face diagonals of a rational cuboid with unit space diagonal are the roots of two cubics whose coefficients are explicit rational functions of two parameters (b,c). Their elliptic curves come from reducibility conditions of those cubics: in 1208.1227 they are the two fixed curves y^2 = -7c^4 + 40c^3 - 84c^2 + 80c - 28 and y^2 = c^4 - 8c^3 + 12c^2 - 16c + 4 (I read the equations from the arXiv HTML), which `12-rs-curves.out` shows both have conductor 32, j = 1728, torsion Z/4 and rank 0; the "parametric families" of 1303.0765 come from a cubic-splitting lemma in the same (b,c)-parametrisation. None of these is a Pythagorean-face fibration, so they are not Peschmann's curves (which have generic j). Asiryan (arXiv:2510.11768), cited in paper 1, uses the rank-0 curve Y^2 = X(X-8)(X-9) to prove irreducibility of Sharipov's cuboid polynomial, again a different question; paper 1's "connection to Q(sqrt 2)" is only the observation that s^4 - 6s^2 + 1 factors over Q(sqrt 2). **verified** for 1208.1227 and Asiryan's curve identity; **could not verify** the exact form of the 1303.0765 families (abstract only).

## Observations the requester might miss

- Every elliptic curve in the three papers is, up to isogeny and up to relabelling (a,b) <-> (m,n), one of de Grey-Gibbs-Helm's EF1-EF4 for the face (p,q) = (m,n): E_{m,n} (paper 3) = EF1 exactly; E_A ~ E_uV ~ EF2; E_3 ~ EF4; E''_A ~ EF1; E'_A ~ E_PQ ~ a fifth curve F2 (quartic (z rho)^2 = R(R+4)(W^2R+4U^2)(V^2R+4U^2), never semistable) which dGGH do not use. The third genus-3 quotient of the genus-5 curve, with private factor E_YS ~ EF3, appears in none of the papers. **verified** at 4-5 test fibers.
- The genus-5 curve X_s = {Rat, Master, Hsum all squares} has J(X_s) ~ EF1 x EF2 x EF3 x EF4 x F2 (Kani-Rosen for the (Z/2)^3 cover of the rho-line; **verified** structurally, numerically at the test fibers). A perfect cuboid with a face of parameters (a,b) is a non-trivial rational point of X_s, and linear Chabauty-Coleman on X_s needs rk EF1 + rk EF2 + rk EF3 + rk EF4 + rk F2 <= 4. Paper 2's method is the special case "one of these ranks is 0"; paper 1 never gets beyond the necessary condition on the quotient C_A.
- On every one of the 2,040 fibers, E_3 has torsion exactly 4 and E_uV exactly 8, so paper 2's Theorem 4.5 reduces in the scanned range to "rank E_3 = 0 or rank E_uV = 0"; the branch "rank EF1 = 0" (no Euler brick at all on the face; dGGH's case 1) is not part of paper 2's pipeline.
- C_A depends only on c^2, so the two (a,b)-fibers s and (s+1)/(s-1) (which swap f_1 and f_2) give the same C_A; e.g. s = 2 and s = 3.
- Sage's `L_ratio` is exactly the requester's proposed "numerical L(E,1) plus denominator bound" route; PARI can replicate it (`ellL1`, `ellisomat` for the isogeny degrees, `elltors`, `omega`), and for conductors below roughly 2,000 PARI's `msfromell`/`mseval` gives an exact modular symbol instead.
- The released data bundle contains `fibers.csv.gz` with rank data for 100,245 fibres (m,n) of the Euler-brick curve EF1 = E_{m,n} (rank 0 on 16,952 + 9,319 fibres, rank 5 on 28).
- The text extraction of the PDFs garbles superscripts (e.g. "13^3" -> "133"); several apparent oddities in the .txt files are artefacts, and the ones that are not (Res(P,Q), EF3/EF4 in dGGH) were confirmed on the PDF pages.
